360 Virtual Tours

Why Every Business Needs a 360° Virtual Tour in 2026

Aug 15, 2026 5 min read JHA360 Team

Most people now decide whether to visit a business before they ever walk through the door. They check Google Maps, skim a few reviews, and look for photos — and increasingly, they look for something more immersive than a handful of static images. That's exactly the gap a 360° virtual tour fills.

It's not just a nice-to-have anymore

A 360° virtual tour lets a potential customer "walk through" your gym, resort, showroom, or restaurant from their phone, right inside Google Maps and Search, before they've made any commitment to visit. That single difference — seeing the real space instead of imagining it from a few photos — changes how much a visitor trusts you before they arrive.

What it actually does for a local business

  • Builds trust before the first visit. Customers walk in already knowing what to expect, which reduces the "is this legit?" hesitation that stops a lot of local searches from converting.
  • Increases time spent on your Google listing. An interactive tour keeps people engaged with your profile longer than a static photo gallery does — and engagement signals feed directly into how Google Maps ranks local results.
  • Differentiates you from competitors instantly. Most local businesses in Maharashtra still only have a handful of low-quality photos on their listing. A professional virtual tour makes you visibly more credible the moment someone compares you side-by-side with the competition.
  • Works as evergreen marketing. Unlike a social media post, a virtual tour keeps working for years — it's live on your Google Business Profile every single day, not just for the 48 hours after you posted it.

Where it matters most

We've seen it make the biggest difference for gyms and fitness studios (letting people see the actual equipment and space before committing to a membership), resorts and hospitality venues (showing the real property instead of a stock-photo-looking website), and showrooms or retail stores (letting shoppers browse the layout before visiting).

Google AI Reviews

How Google's AI Review Summaries Are Changing Local Search

Aug 22, 2026 6 min read JHA360 Team

If you've searched for a local business on Google recently, you may have noticed something new above the individual reviews: a short AI-written summary pulling out common themes — "customers consistently mention fast service, friendly staff, and clean facilities" — instead of a wall of star ratings you have to read through yourself. This is Google's AI review summary feature, and by mid-2026 it's live on the majority of Google Business Profiles.

What's actually changed

Google now uses AI to synthesize the themes running through your reviews, so a searcher gets the gist in seconds instead of scrolling through dozens of individual comments. A related feature, "Know Before You Go," goes even further — pulling from reviews, photos, and your profile information to tell a prospective customer what to expect: typical wait times, popular items, atmosphere, and accessibility, all before they've clicked through to your full listing.

Google has also begun testing AI-drafted reply suggestions for business owners responding to reviews inside the Business Profile dashboard — you still read, edit, and manually approve every reply, but the first draft is generated for you.

Why this raises the stakes on review quality

When a human skims your reviews, one or two bad ones can get lost in the noise. When an AI summarizes them, patterns matter much more than individual comments — a recurring theme in your reviews, good or bad, is exactly what gets surfaced to the top of your listing. That means:

  • Generic reviews add little value. "Great place!" gives the AI nothing to work with. Reviews that mention specifics — what someone bought, how a staff member helped them, what made the visit smooth — are what shape a favorable summary.
  • Recency and volume both matter more now. A profile with a steady flow of detailed, recent reviews earns a noticeably richer AI summary than one with a handful of old, vague ones.
  • Incomplete profiles get worse outcomes. If your Business Profile is thin, Google may fill gaps in its AI-generated summaries using whatever it can find elsewhere — your website, third-party listings — which isn't always accurate. Keeping your own profile complete keeps you in control of your own narrative.

What to actually do about it

Encourage customers to leave specific, honest reviews rather than just a star rating. Respond to reviews consistently — positive and negative — since the substance of your responses feeds into the same signals. And keep your Business Profile itself detailed and current, since AI Overviews and Maps summaries increasingly pull directly from profile completeness, not just review content.

Local SEO

Google Business Profile SEO: The Complete Guide for Local Businesses

Aug 29, 2026 7 min read JHA360 Team

If you still call it "Google My Business," you're not alone — Google renamed it Google Business Profile (GBP) a while back, but the free listing itself is more important than ever. It's usually the very first thing a potential customer sees about you, often before they ever visit your website. Here's how to actually optimize it.

Google ranks local results on three factors

Relevance, distance, and prominence. Relevance is how well your profile matches what someone searched for; distance is simply how close you are to the searcher; prominence is a mix of your review volume/quality, profile completeness, and general online visibility. You can't do much about distance, but the other two are entirely in your control.

The core checklist

  • Pick the right primary category — and add relevant secondary categories. This single field has an outsized effect on which searches you show up for.
  • Fill out every attribute field — parking, accessibility, payment options, service area, amenities. Conversational searches ("gym near me open late with parking") increasingly match directly against these fields.
  • Add products and services with real descriptions. Even service businesses benefit — a salon can list its service menu, a firm can list flat-fee packages. Each entry acts as its own mini landing page inside your profile.
  • Post regularly. Google Posts (offers, updates, events) feed directly into how fresh and active your profile looks — both to users and to Google's ranking systems.
  • Upload real, high-quality photos consistently — not just once at setup. Profiles with recent, genuine photos are treated as more trustworthy than static ones.
  • Add a 360° virtual tour. This is one of the most underused parts of a GBP listing — most competitors only have a handful of static photos, so a full walkthrough tour is an easy way to stand out.
  • Respond to every review, quickly and specifically. Aim for within 24 hours. Generic replies help less than you'd think — specific, genuine responses do more for both trust and AI review summaries (see our post on Google's AI review summaries above).
  • Keep hours, contact info, and service areas current. Inconsistent or outdated information is one of the fastest ways to lose ranking and customer trust simultaneously.

Verification matters more than it used to

Google has tightened verification — video verification is now the primary method for most business types, largely to cut down on spam and fraudulent listings, which have been a particular problem in major Indian cities. A properly verified, fully built-out profile is treated very differently from a thin, unverified one.

Business Ranking

Why Business Ranking on Google Matters More Than Ever

Sep 5, 2026 5 min read JHA360 Team

Nearly half of all Google searches now have local intent — someone looking for a business "near me," in their neighborhood, or in a specific part of their city. If you're not ranking in the local results (the map pack) for the searches your customers are actually making, you're effectively invisible to a huge share of the people who would otherwise become your customers.

Local search behavior moves fast

Most people who do a local search on their phone visit or contact that business within a day. That's a very short window between "discovered you on Google" and "decided whether to become your customer" — and it means your ranking position, your reviews, and your profile completeness are doing an enormous amount of the persuading before a human ever speaks to you.

What actually drives ranking

  • Profile completeness. A fully filled-out Google Business Profile consistently outperforms a thin one — often dramatically — in both visibility and click-through.
  • Review volume and quality. Both how many reviews you have and what they actually say matter, especially now that AI summaries surface the themes in your reviews directly in search results.
  • Engagement signals. How long people spend on your profile, whether they click through to your website, request directions, or view your photos and virtual tour — all of it feeds back into how prominent Google considers you.
  • Consistency. Matching business information (name, address, phone number) across your website, Google Business Profile, and other directories remains one of the most basic and most overlooked ranking factors.

The compounding effect

Ranking well isn't a one-time achievement — it compounds. A business that ranks higher gets more visibility, which drives more clicks and more reviews, which improves ranking further. The businesses that treat their Google presence as a passive, "set it up once" listing are the ones that get gradually outranked by competitors actively maintaining theirs — posting regularly, responding to every review, and keeping their profile visually rich with real photos and virtual tours.

In practice, this means your Google Business Profile deserves the same ongoing attention as your actual storefront. It's very often the first impression a customer forms of your business — before your website, before a phone call, before they've spoken to anyone on your team.
